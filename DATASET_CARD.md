## What this is
A 3-class NLI-style dataset (contradiction / consistent / neutral) of clause pairs for
detecting conflicts between Maharashtra Government Resolutions. Every pair is grounded
in a **real seed clause** from the project corpus (archival GR PDFs); the counterpart
clause and the label are **LLM-generated locally (Ollama llama3.1-8B)** or produced by
**deterministic augmentation/mining** (number-swap contradictions, truncation
consistents, mined neutrals) — the exact provenance is recorded per pair in the
`generator` field. No pairs were generated by Gemini or Groq.

## Generation
- Prompt: see `src/finetune/generate_pairs.py` (`GEN_PROMPT`) — verbatim, versioned in git.
- Contradiction types (tagged per pair): amount · date · eligibility · authority · scope.
- Anti-artifact rules: ~half of contradictions fully rephrased (low lexical overlap);
  consistent/neutral classes mirror the overlap spread; hard-negative neutrals reuse
  scheme vocabulary; premise/hypothesis order randomized post-hoc.
- Dedup: exact pair-text match (validate_dataset.py).
- Split: **grouped by seed clause** (`split_dataset.py`, seed 42). Honest caveat:
  composite-seed mined pairs and duplicate corpus clauses mean **29.8% of test rows
  share ONE clause side verbatim with a train row** (0 identical full pairs). The
  eval table therefore reports fine-tuned F1 on both the full test set (0.9134) and
  the zero-overlap subset (0.8957, n=395) — see `results/metrics_leakfree.json`.

## Distributions (from validate_dataset.py, final overnight build 2026-07-27)
- **Pairs: 4,490** (after exact-dedup) → split 3,633 train / 294 dev / 563 test (grouped by seed clause; see split caveat above)
- Labels: contradiction 1,796 · consistent 1,364 · neutral 1,330
- Languages: mr-en cross-lingual 3,290 · mr 886 · en 314
- Generators: ollama:llama3.1 3,604 · augment-numswap 350 · mined-neutral 218 · augment-truncation 297 · mined-neutral-hard 21
- Mean lexical overlap (Jaccard) by class: contradiction 0.211 · consistent 0.167 · neutral 0.015
- **TF-IDF + LogReg probe macro-F1: 0.788** (random-split *dataset audit* over all pairs;
  threshold 0.75 — exceeded). Interpretation: with the dominant cross-lingual construction,
  the residual signal is LLM English vocabulary style per label, inherent to
  single-generator data. The eval table's lexical-baseline row is a separate,
  **protocol-matched** run (fit on train, scored on the held-out test set: **0.7754**,
  `src/finetune/tfidf_baseline.py`); the fine-tuned model's +0.14 margin above it is the
  semantic-learning evidence. A human blind-check of 50 stratified test pairs from the
  **readable subset** (legacy-font garble makes some clauses unjudgeable by humans;
  sampler: `src/finetune/make_blind_check.py`) is reported in EVAL_TABLE.md.

## Known limitations (read this before trusting the numbers)
1. **All labels are LLM-generated, and we measured how wrong they are.** A 50-pair
   human blind check (readable subset) + independent blind adjudication of every
   disagreement puts **label noise at ~26%** in that sample — mostly the 8B
   generator's contradiction/consistent labels (polarity-reversed "restatements",
   off-topic "contradictions"); neutral labels ≈ 6% noise. The model scores 0.92
   against its own labels on those pairs but **0.70 against adjudicated truth** —
   both numbers are reported (EVAL_TABLE.md, "Label quality"). There was no
   legal-expert review. A validated cross-family relabeling (`relabel_test_gemini.py`,
   Gemini at 0.88 vs adjudicated truth) independently confirms the estimate:
   0.6975 vs cleaned labels on 287 pairs vs 0.8851 vs the originals on the same subset.
2. **Shared provenance**: all LLM pairs come from a single generator (llama3.1-8B),
   so the Groq Llama-3.3 judge shares the generator's model family (contaminated
   cell), while the Gemini judge shares no family with it (cross-family cell). The
   generator itself scores 0.499 zero-shot on the same test set — the fine-tuned
   student beating its teacher by +0.41 is the strongest anti-memorization evidence.
3. The underlying corpus text passed through legacy-font extraction repair
   (`src/ingest/clean_corpus.py`); digits were audited to survive verbatim, but
   residual script noise may remain in some seed clauses.
4. Marathi pairs are machine-generated Marathi; register may differ from drafted GR prose.
5. Source clauses come from public government documents (archival copies).
